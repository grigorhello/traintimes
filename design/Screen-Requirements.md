# Train Times v1 — Screen Requirements

Nov 5, 2026 · @Grigor

## Purpose and scope

This document specifies how every version 1 screen of the GO Train iPhone app behaves, so it can be built in SwiftUI without further design decisions. The visual source is the design canvas [GO Train App — iPhone v1](https://claude.ai/code/artifact/5ad8642b-4e0b-4d23-b6f5-fb800acbb10e) and the design system [GO Train App](https://claude.ai/code/artifact/fefd0b1c-3c6f-446d-9bf8-874bf1252452); the product source is the GO Train App Product Design Brief v1 (4 Nov 2026). Where this document and the brief differ, this document wins: the Schedule screen is a calendar-style timeline, not the brief's row list.

The product promise is unchanged: know when your train leaves, whether it is delayed, and when something changes. Every decision below serves a hurried rider using one hand, who should read the next departure and its status in about three seconds.

| In version 1 | Out of version 1 |
| --- | --- |
| Direct GO trains between two stations, scheduled departures and arrivals, a date choice of up to 42 days | Login, payment, subscription, advertising, sponsor placement |
| Saved last route and the last three routes opened (Recent), kept on device (D48) | AI chat, live train maps, ticketing, refunds |
| Live status: on time, delayed, cancelled, scheduled only, stale, offline | Bus planning, UP Express, transfer routing, crowd reports |
| Published platform, or a dash pill until GO posts it (D82) | Calendar access, Apple Watch app |
| Trip details with stop list and arrival basis | Delay threshold settings or any advanced alert options |
| One-off and repeating weekday alerts, anonymous, server monitored, delivered by APNs | Any claim of real-time, instant or guaranteed delivery |
| Light and dark appearance, Dynamic Type, VoiceOver, 12/24-hour time |  |
| Home Screen widgets Next train and Departures, and Lock Screen Next train (D101, TT-181 to TT-185) |  |
| On board: a ride followed by the phone's location, started with "I’m on board", Yes on "On board?", or by itself when you leave with your alerted train (D60, D110, D120); the location stays on the phone |  |

All station names, times, platforms, train numbers and stop lists shown on the canvas are illustrative fixtures. Fixtures stay isolated from production data and are never shown as live service.

## Navigation and shared rules

The app has three bottom tabs, Schedule, Alerts and Settings, and nothing else at the top level (D48). On iOS 26 and later the bar is a floating Liquid Glass capsule with icons only (a tram, a bell, a gear; the system draws the selection), and content scrolls under it with a soft fade (D49); earlier iOS keeps the opaque bar with the same icons. Tapping the Schedule tab while Schedule shows scrolls the timeline to Now and returns to Today, so there is no Now button anywhere (D49). Trip details, alert setup and line notices open as native sheets (`.sheet`); choosing a station never does, the picker unfolds under the route card (D54), so the timetable is always one dismiss away. Before the first timetable there is no tutorial, permission request or carousel: a cold start plays the one-second Bloom splash from the launch still, and the first launch shows the welcome and two taps (D94).

**Trip identity never changes.** A trip is keyed by service date plus the feed's stable trip id, never by departure time. In every label and notification the train is named by its scheduled origin departure: the 7:32 AM train stays "7:32 AM train" when its estimate moves to 7:41 AM. Do not create a new trip when an estimate moves.

**Status is always colour plus word plus icon**, from this closed set:

| Status label | When it applies | Colour tokens | SF Symbol |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| On time | Fresh live evidence or a fresh applicable prediction with delay under 3 min | ontime-ink on ontime-fill | checkmark.circle.fill |
| Delayed N min | Reported departure delay of at least 3 min | delay-ink on delay-fill | clock.badge.exclamationmark |
| Cancelled | Trip cancelled in the feed | cancel-ink on cancel-fill | xmark.octagon.fill |
| Stop skipped | Selected origin or destination skipped | cancel-ink on cancel-fill | xmark.octagon.fill |
| Scheduled | Offline only (D49: a train with no live status reads On time) | scheduled-ink on scheduled-fill (neutral grey) | clock |
| Last reported HH:MM | Live data older than the freshness threshold (proposed 3 min, tune against the feed) | scheduled-ink on scheduled-fill | clock |
| Offline | No connection; cached schedule shown | scheduled-ink on scheduled-fill | wifi.slash |

Missing or stale data is never reported as live: a train with no live status reads Scheduled (D12, D13, D50), and the status row says when data is stale or offline. Express is not a status: on a card it is the lightning bolt riders already know from GO's trip planner (SF Symbol bolt.fill) right after the departure time, accent when the stopping-pattern rule marks the train express and a grey struck bolt otherwise (D50). Express is the only amenity on a card.

**Freshness** is one line that states the data mode, shown on Schedule only when there is something to say (D49): "Loading timetable", "Last reported 7:29 AM · showing scheduled times" (stale), "Offline · last updated 6:52 AM" (cached). Live data and timetable-only data show no line. Trip details keeps the full set ("Updated just now", "Scheduled times only"). Refresh on foreground, on pull to refresh, and on a periodic interval agreed with the data provider; a refresh keeps row identity and scroll position and updates the line quietly.

**Time and date.** Times follow the device 12/24-hour setting unless overridden in Settings, use America/Toronto, and respect daylight-saving changes. The service day is the GO service day: trips after midnight belong to the previous service day and sort after 11:59 PM. The Schedule screen itself turns to the next calendar day at 12:00 AM Toronto time, not at the 3:00 AM service cutoff (D52): the day strip, the selected day and the timeline all move together, and an app opened at 12:01 AM shows the actual day, selected, from its top. Every arrival is labelled "Arrival" on a Schedule card ("Scheduled arrival" or "Est. arrival" on Trip details); an estimate is shown only when the feed supplies or reliably supports one.

**Copy.** Sentence case everywhere, Canadian spelling (favourite, colour, cancelled), no exclamation marks, no emoji (except warm lines such as the arrival wishes, which end with one, D126), no promises: write "Send updates as changes are reported", never "Never miss a train".

## Schedule screen (timeline)

The Schedule tab is a vertical, to-scale timeline of today's direct trains for the saved route, with the next usable train emphasised and the current time marked. Top to bottom: the line (code tile in the GO line colour, 26 pt bold name, amber Notice chip at the right end) above the route card (no app name above it, D48, D49); day strip; status row, only when loading, offline or stale; optional watched-trip strip; the timeline, which scrolls under the glass tab bar. Three departures fit on a 390 pt phone at default text size in the normal state.

**Route card** (D48). A card drawn like a maps route box: a hollow ring for the origin, three dots, and an accent pin for the destination down the left; origin over destination in `headline`, one 48 pt row each split by a hairline, on one line (long names shrink a little rather than truncate); no "From"/"To" words and no chevrons. Tapping a station makes that row the search field and unfolds the station picker under the card (D54). Swap (`arrow.up.arrow.down`) is a 44 pt Liquid Glass button (a raised surface before iOS 26) to the right of both rows. A swap is the rider's tap; the route also turns by itself when the app opens at its other station (Automatic direction, D116, under Route form and station picker).

**Line row** (D49). Above the route card: a 32 pt rounded tile with the line's code (LW, LE, MI, KI, RH, BR, ST) in white on the GO line colour, then the line's name in 26 pt bold, ink. Colours follow GO's own, which riders already know: Lakeshore West #8B1A31, Lakeshore East #EA3624, Milton #E6853E, Kitchener #3A8346, Richmond Hill #4397C3, Barrie #143664, Stouffville #724715; a line outside the table uses the feed's own colour, else ink-secondary. When a line-wide notice applies, an amber "Notice" chip (warning glyph and the word, 44 pt target) sits at the right end of the row and opens the notice in a sheet; the notice is dismissible per notice id and never changes a trip's status.

**Day strip** (D49). A horizontal carousel of 60 × 48 pt tiles with 12 pt radius, the weekday over the day number, with a 20 pt margin at the leading edge. The strip opens with Today first, reading "Today"; every day the schedule covers follows (the feed's calendar, 42 days at most; 14 when the schedule has no end), and five tiles show in full with the sixth peeking under a 16 pt fade so the strip clearly scrolls. The 7 days before Today sit to the left of Today, a swipe to the right away; yesterday's tile peeks into the leading margin (D53). A past day opens like any other day (scheduled times only, no Now line, from its first train) and shows the "No direct trains" state when the timetable does not cover it. The selected day is solid accent with on-accent text; others are surface tiles; while another day is selected, "Today" turns accent green so the way back is visible. Selecting any day other than today shows scheduled times only, hides the Now line, and starts the timeline at the first train of that service day. Selecting a tile that is out of view scrolls the strip to it. There is no depart-after filter; looking ahead is scrolling or picking a day.

**Weather tile** (D105, TT-226). Pinned at the left of the day strip row, not scrolling: 80 × 48 pt, radius 12, a day tile's surface and shadow, a 28 pt coloured weather glyph and the temperature (17 pt semibold), no words. It shows the From station's weather for the selected day (Today: now; another day: the high) and changes at once with the day. Rain or snow when a 40 % chance is expected later that day, wind for gusts of 50 km/h or more, else the sky. A past day, 10 or more days ahead, or offline with nothing saved today: a flat grey tile with a dash that opens nothing. A tap opens a small sheet: station and day, the temperature at 48 pt with one line, three facts, six hours and Apple's Weather attribution. The days scroll beside it, four in view.

**Status row** (D49). One line under the day strip, only while the timetable loads, offline or stale (see Freshness). There is no row, and no Now button, while data is live or timetable-only. Tapping the Schedule tab again scrolls to Now and selects Today in one tap.

**Timeline geometry.**

| Element | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Scale | 7 pt per minute at default text size; gridline every 15 min, each labelled with its time in grey footnote text on the left |
| Now line | 2 pt accent line across the timeline with a dot on the rail and a "Now" label; moves once a minute. The only accent on the rail (D80). Plays once when the timeline shows: the line draws across from the rail, the dot sends out two rings, "Now" pops (0.9 s; a fade under Reduce Motion) |
| Initial scroll | The app opens already scrolled so Now sits about 5 min (35 pt) below the top of the timeline, once the cards are measured; earlier trains are above and reachable by scrolling up. The scroll repeats shortly after, so it lands even when the content was laid out late, and stops repeating the moment the rider drags (D52) |
| Train marker | A dot on the rail at the train's expected departure (scheduled when no estimate): 12 pt ink-tertiary ring and connector for a train, the same ring for the next usable train, whose connector carries an ink glint running from the rail into the card every 1.6 s (plain ink under Reduce Motion), so no train mark is green (D80) |
| Left column | Gridline times and the Now label only (D49): train times are on the cards, so no train has a time pill in the column |
| Connector | 14 pt, 2 pt horizontal line from marker to card in the marker's colour |
| Gridline labels | Hidden within 22 pt of the Now label; the gridline itself stays |
| Card anchor | The card's departure time line is vertically centred on its marker |
| Minimum gap | Cards never touch: at least 16 pt between cards. If two trains are closer than that at base scale, the timeline zooms in for that segment only, and the gridlines in it follow the zoom |
| Gap label | No labels between cards; the spacing itself conveys the gap. Only a fold carries a label |
| Fold | A gap over 30 min collapses to a dashed 30-min segment with a pill stating the real gap and the next train ("No trains for 48 min · next 6:40 PM") |
| Day end | The timeline continues to the last train of the service day, then an end-of-service line |

**Delay rendering.** A delayed train keeps a hollow amber marker at its scheduled time; an amber 4 pt bar runs down the rail to the expected-time marker, so the bar's length is the delay. The card sits at the expected time and shows the expected time and the estimated arrival in amber with the Delayed N min status; the scheduled time stays in Trip details (D50).

**Cancellation rendering.** A cancelled train keeps its scheduled slot with a red x marker, struck times in ink-tertiary, the red Cancelled status and the text "Next usable below"; it has no bell and no bolt. It is never the lead and is excluded from usable-departure logic and gap labels.

**Departed trains** (D86, TT-203). A train has left when its expected departure (scheduled plus delay) is at or before now; where GO's train feed places the train, it has left when it moves off instead (D114, below). Its card goes flat and grey: canvas-coloured surface, a 1 pt hairline outline instead of the shadow, pressed to 0.96, time, platform, trip length, arrival, bolt and bell at 50 % with no colour, and the status line a grey dot and "Departed" at full ink-secondary (a cancelled train keeps its red x, struck times and "Cancelled"). Its rail mark is a 6 pt solid ink-tertiary dot with an ink-tertiary connector. On Today every departed card sits 88 pt above its timetable place, so the last one ends 4 pt above the Now line at its minute and the Now line never runs behind a departed card; its dot and connector sit 30 pt above its time, level with its bottom row. On a past day every card has the flat look in its own place. At the minute tick (or live update) that makes a train leave, with the timeline on screen, the step up plays once (about 1.05 s, the Departed-A2 board): the Now line glides onto the ring and its dot swells with one ring, the card presses back and rises while its ring follows and fills, "Departed" cross-fades in (the lead's "Departing" and glint stay until the card moves, then fade with it), the Now rule draws out from the rail once the card has cleared it, and the next card lifts to 1.03 and springs back as its time grows to 34 pt and its countdown and glint start. Never on launch, a day change, scrolling or coming back to the tab or the app; under Reduce Motion a 280 ms cross-fade.

**Boarding at the platform** (D114, TT-233; Card states canvas, boards 1 to 4). While GO's train feed places a train on Today within 350 m of the From station, not more than 2 min ago, and its platform is posted, its card says so: the platform pill filled (accent ground, on-accent number) and the status row "Boarding" in the accent with a filled dot that pings every 1.6 s (a halo growing 7 pt out while it fades; none under Reduce Motion). The card keeps the ordinary surface, border and shadow: no accent outline or tint, so two trains boarding at once do not read as selected (TT-248, D125). The countdown stays ("in 8 min", then "Departing" in the last minute and for as long as the train stands there past its time); a late train keeps its amber times and says Boarding. The status row and the pill cross-fade into Boarding (`TTMotion.cross`). The train stays the next train and its card stays in place, not stepped up, until it leaves: it has left when the From station drops out of GO's updates for the trip (counted from 1 min before its time) or when its position is more than 350 m away once its time has come, and the departed step up plays then. With no position newer than 2 min (none for the trip, offline, another day, fixtures) nothing changes: the card reads "On time" or its delay, "Departing", and Departed at its time. A train at the station whose platform is not posted yet is not Boarding, and not Departed while it stands there. Cancelled trains and skipped stops never board.

**The train you ride** (D130, TT-256; Card states canvas, boards 6 to 9, option A). While a ride runs, the card of its train on Today, where Schedule shows the ride's two stations in the ride's direction, says "You’re on board" in the status row: the train glyph (16 pt) and the words in the accent, in the status line's 15 pt semibold, where the dot and status were. The card keeps the ordinary surface, colours and size (no grey, no press back, no outline or tint), though it keeps a departed train's place above Now and its small rail dot once it has left. Its arrival and ride length follow the ride: the expected arrival at your stop, amber when the ride is 1 min or more late ("29 min ride", "Arrival 4:58 PM"). The mark cross-fades in and out as the ride starts or ends (`TTMotion.cross`). A cancelled train or skipped stop is never marked. Above it, the ride strip names the train on a line of its own: the train glyph and "You’re on the 4:29 PM" in the accent (the scheduled departure, D9), over the strip's ride line; the strip is 62 pt high.

**Card contents** (D50). Three fixed rows, 118 pt in every state, so nothing moves between states; padding 14 / 10 / 14 / 16, rows 4 pt apart. Row 1 (38 pt): the departure time (34 pt semibold for the next usable train, 32 pt for others, tabular numerals), the Express bolt 8 pt after it (16 pt in an 18 pt box: accent when express, control-border with a slash when not), and the bell flush right (a 26 pt glyph in a 48 pt target, filled accent when the alert is on). Row 2 (20 pt): on the next usable train only, "Platform" and the platform pill (D82: the posted number bold in the accent on an accent tint, or a dash on a grey pill until GO posts it) (15 pt label, 14 pt pill), then, once the number is posted, a dot and minutes until departure in ink semibold ("in 11 min", "Departing" under 1 min, updating each minute; nothing beside the dash, D125); the platform comes before the countdown (D82). On the next usable train from 15 min to 1 min before it leaves, with fresh live data and a feed that posts platforms, the dash pill pulses 1 → 0.4 over 1.6 s (static with Reduce Motion) and the number replaces the dash with a 0.2 s cross-fade (D55); a cancelled card shows "Next usable below" instead. The trip length sits right in 13 pt ink-secondary ("40 min ride" up to 99 min, then "1 h 45 min ride", D81). Row 3 (24 pt): the status line, a 10 pt dot and the word in 15 pt semibold in the status colour (filled for live information; a ring and "Scheduled" for no live data, stale and offline, which the status row names; no pill, no icon), and right "Arrival" in 13 pt ink-secondary before the arrival time in 17 pt semibold. Delayed: both times in delay-ink. Cancelled or stop skipped: both times struck in ink-tertiary, no bolt, no bell. There is no "Departs" label: the rail says it. Cards are 18 pt radius surface cards with the light-mode card shadow.

**Interactions.** Tap the card to open Trip details; tap the bell to arm an alert for that train in one tap (Alert setup and notification permission); a cancelled card has no bell. Pull to refresh keeps card identity and scroll position. Scrolling is continuous through the service day. A delayed train stays visible past its scheduled time until a reliable departure event or updated estimate shows it has left; the passage of time alone is not evidence of departure.

**Sorting.** Usable departures are ordered by expected departure when available, otherwise scheduled departure. Only direct services that stop at both selected stations in the correct order are shown.

A delayed train stays on the timeline past its scheduled time until the feed reports a departure or an updated estimate; it must not disappear because the clock passed 7:32. A cancelled train stays in its slot for the rest of the service day.

## Route form and station picker

On first launch the splash becomes the welcome (the network picture, "Welcome to Train Times", "Choose your line"), then step 1 (your line) and step 2 (your station, under Schedule's own header); the station opens Schedule and the route is saved automatically. A tile on the picture skips step 1. No account or permission comes first, and "Search stations" opens the compact route form below (D94). There is no separate station search page and no route sheet (D54): a tap on a station row makes that row the search field, in place, and unfolds the suggestions under the route card. First use and Schedule use the same card and panel (frames: *Route form · first use*, *picking the starting station*, *picking the destination*, *Schedule · changing a station in place*).

**Route form** (the first screen, under a large title). Title "Choose a route". The route card (see Schedule, D48): origin row then destination row, and a single 44 pt swap button to the right of both rows. Below it one primary button, "Show trains", a tinted glass capsule on iOS 26, enabled only when both stations are set and different. On first use the rows show the placeholders "Choose starting station" and "Choose destination" in ink-tertiary, the button is disabled, and picking From moves to the destination row at once with the panel still open, so the first route takes two picks and one tap. A footnote says "No account or permission is needed. The route is saved on this device and opens next time." "Show trains" and the footnote are hidden while a station row is the field.

**The picker.** The tapped row keeps its look and shows an accent cursor (on first use the placeholder stays until the rider types; on Schedule the current station shows as a grey hint with the cursor before it and nothing selected, so the first keystroke replaces it); the keyboard rises; the swap button becomes an X (Cancel). The list is one surface with the card (D54): the same fill and radius with no gap, one shadow around both, a hairline seam inset 16 pt where they join. It sits at the card's width, ends 10 pt above the keyboard, is as tall as its rows and scrolls past that, ending under a 28 pt fade (a short list ends 6 pt under its last row). Groups, each a 13 pt ink-secondary title over 56 pt rows with hairlines inset 16 pt: **Recent** (Schedule only, before typing; D48: the last three routes opened, newest first, leaving out the route on screen, each with mini route markers, origin over destination at 15 pt semibold and the line's code tile at the right; a tap shows that route at once), **Your stations** (stations in the recent routes, with a clock icon) and **All stations** alphabetically. When choosing To, Union Station GO is listed first as a suggestion unless it is the From station. Every row has a leading glyph in a 20 pt column (mini route markers on Recent, a clock on Your stations, a ring for From or a pin for To on All stations and typed results) and no trailing clock. Each station row shows the full name in `headline` and its line context in `footnote` ("Lakeshore West", "All lines"); names are never abbreviated. The station chosen for the other field is greyed with "Same as To" (or "Same as From") and cannot be selected; the active field's own station is not listed. Typing filters every group by word prefix, case and accent insensitive ("oak", "union st"); Recent hides while typing; a single group of matches is titled "Stations"; no match shows one row, "No stations match". The Return key picks the first match.

**On Schedule** a pick applies at once: the route is saved and the timeline reloads, Recent updates and the panel folds. A frosted veil (a material under a cool tint) covers the timeline while the picker is open (its top edge eases in over 40 pt, so no hard line shows beside the card), the line row stays crisp above it and the day strip fades out. Cancel is the X, a tap on the veil or a swipe down on the panel; nothing changes. The list is revealed by a clip growing down from the card's bottom edge while its rows fall in one after another with a little bounce (about 0.6 s; the swap button turns into the X the same way), and folds back in 0.22 s with no stagger; with Reduce Motion it only fades. Picking a row gives a selection haptic and a press tint that holds for 120 ms before the list closes (D54).

**Swap** reverses From and To on the rider's tap and reloads the timeline.

**Automatic direction** (TT-234, D116; it replaces "The app never reverses a route on its own based on time of day or location"). The saved route turns to leave from the station the rider is closer to. When Schedule comes to the front on Today with nothing over it, the app takes one look at the phone's location, once per visit (a cold start or a return from the background), and compares it on the phone with the route's two stations. Clearly at the To station (the other station at least 1.5 times as far, and the closer one within 15 km or the phone on its outer side): From becomes that station, the trains are the new direction's, and 1 s later Schedule's glass capsule (where the alert capsule shows, D71; a bell's capsule wins over it) reads "Now from Union" with Undo for 5 s. The route turns by itself only when the answer is in before the trains can be read: by the moment the launch splash lifts on a cold start, within 1 s on coming back. A later answer (the look stops after 10 s), one after a touch on Schedule, or a reading on the track more than 1 km before the station moves nothing, and the capsule asks "Trains from Union?" with Switch; left alone, nothing changes. Already at the From station, in between, far from both, another day, a ride, or a sheet, the picker, the train question or a bell's capsule over Schedule: nothing changes and nothing is said. Undo turns the route back, the capsule then reads "Won’t switch by itself for an hour", and the route is left alone between those two stations for that hour; once the capsule is gone the swap button is the Undo. A swap or a station pick by hand is kept until the app has been out of view for more than 10 min. A ride that followed the phone's location to the rider's stop and ended there turns the route to leave from that stop, with no new reading, when that stop is the route's To station and the ride ended within the last hour. The capsules use the short station name, and only the name is cut when the line is too long ("Trains from Brampton Inno…?"). Never by time of day and never in the background. The app never asks for location by itself: the look runs only while location is already allowed with Precise Location and Settings › Automatic direction is on. **The turn in motion** (TT-235, D116; boards 14 and 16): the two names trade rows as on a tap of the swap button, in a calm form: the arrows turn about 6° past upright, the button does not press and the pin does not pop. From 310 ms the From ring cross-fades (180 ms) into the accent location arrow, with no rings round it; the arrow stays for the visit, across tabs and sheets, and goes when the app goes to the background, on Undo, and when the route changes by hand or by a link. On a cold start the route turns under the splash, the card keeps the old order at rest, and the names trade once the splash has gone (the lift plus its fade: 200 ms, 240 ms on the first 8 launches); on coming back the turn starts at once. The timeline is never empty: the other direction's trains are read first, while the old ones show; then the old cards fade out in 200 ms as the new ones cascade in, the timeline is at Now and Now plays once. Trains not read in time (by the lift, or within the 1 s) count as a late answer: nothing moves and the capsule asks. Undo and Switch play the same calm turn. With Reduce Motion the names cross-fade in 0.18 s and the arrow is simply there. The turn plays no haptic and sends no analytics event. The one-time strip and the quiet after 8 turns are TT-236.

**Validation and persistence.** Identical stations are rejected at the picker (the greyed row), and inline for a saved route that has the same station twice. ## Notification rules and copy

A small server monitors active rules against the live feed and sends visible notifications through APNs; background polling on the phone is never responsible for timely detection. These are product defaults, not GO Transit service guarantees, and user-facing copy stays realistic: "as changes are reported", never "real-time" or "guaranteed".

1. Monitor a watched trip from 60 min before its scheduled departure until a reliable departure event; if the trip is delayed, keep monitoring past the scheduled time.
2. When a rider enables an alert inside that window and a qualifying disruption is already known, send it once immediately.
3. Notify on: a reported departure delay of 3 min or more; a cancellation; a skipped origin or destination. A line-wide notice alone never becomes a trip cancellation.
4. Send a further delay update only when the reported delay changes by 5 min or more, the cancellation status changes, or the train recovers to under 3 min late after a delay alert. Coalesce rapid feed changes within 60 s; never send two identical messages.
5. Keep the scheduled departure as the trip identity in every message.
6. Set APNs expiry to the end of the useful trip window (scheduled departure plus 2 h) so stale delay warnings are discarded. Stop departure-delay alerts after departure; arrival information stays in-app on the watched-trip strip.
7. For a repeating rule, resolve the actual trip for each local service date. If the schedule change removes or moves the selected train, set the rule to Review needed and send one notification; never attach the rule to a different train silently.
8. On a selected weekday with no matching service, show "No matching train today" in the Alerts row; send nothing. A normal unselected day stays quiet.
9. Rules are keyed to an anonymous installation id and device token; no account. Deleting a rule or the app removes it from the server within one monitoring cycle.
10. Tapping a notification opens Trip details for that trip id and service date, even if the app was closed. A notification for a past service date opens the Alerts tab instead.

## Brand: Train Times

The product is called Train Times. The name says exactly what the app is, in the words riders type into the App Store search box. It replaced an earlier abstract name (Nowline) that failed the one-second test: a rider must understand the icon and the name at a glance. GO appears only as a description of what the app shows, never as the brand. The App Store title is Train Times: GO train schedule (30 characters, the limit), the subtitle is Today's GO trains and delays, and the home-screen label is Train Times (11 characters, which fits under an iOS icon without truncation). The bare name Train Times belongs to a UK rail app, which is why the store title carries the qualifier; the title must be confirmed in App Store Connect, and a short trademark check is advisable. The four brand boards (mark and app icon, lockup and wordmark, App Store product page, App Store search result) sit above the Schedule row on the canvas and carry the SVG sources.

**Mark and app icon.** A white train head on brand green #0C7B53: cab 64 × 62 units with 18-unit top corners and 10-unit bottom corners, windscreen 44 × 22, two lights 12 in diameter, a rail 52 × 7 beneath. Three flat shapes, no gradient, shadow or outline; it reads as a train at 29 px. Dark icon: mint #6EE7B7 on #0B1F17. Tinted icon: the same shapes in white on black. The contained symbol takes the current text colour and is the in-app glyph (a 22 pt mask) in Settings › About. The icon never carries letters (a white G or GO on green is GO Transit's mark), GO's olive green, GO or Metrolinx logos or livery, status colours, or an added clock or badge.

**Colour.** Brand green replaces indigo as the accent everywhere; the values are in Visual tokens and accessibility. Because green is now the brand, On time is a green badge in the accent (D49: a neutral grey badge looked disabled), delays are amber and cancellations red; Scheduled (no live data, another day, stale, offline) is grey (D50); the On state of an alert rule uses the accent. Light accent #0C7B53 on white measures 5.3:1; dark accent #6EE7B7 on the dark surface measures above 10:1.

**Wordmark and lockup.** Train Times in SF Pro Display Semibold, sentence case, two words. ## Assumptions and open questions

Start with fixtures and a transport-data abstraction, then connect verified sources; the items below are what still needs confirming before live status and alerts ship.

- [ ] Metrolinx GO API access: confirm credentials, permitted refresh rate, trip identifiers, cancellation semantics, platform availability and timestamps for the real-time feed. Live endpoints were not validated in the research behind the brief.
- [ ] Freshness threshold: 3 min is the proposed point where live data is demoted to "Last reported"; tune against observed feed update intervals.
- [ ] Estimated arrival: confirm the feed provides a reliable destination estimate; until it does, destination arrival is always labelled Scheduled.
- [ ] Express flag: confirm how express or limited-stop services are marked; show the tag only when verified.
- [ ] Line notices: confirm the service-alert feed and how a notice is scoped to a line; it is shown as the Notice chip on the line row, never as a trip status.
- [x] Schedule horizon: closed (D35, D49): the strip covers every day of the feed's calendar, 42 days at most.
- [ ] Data attribution and independent-app wording: use the provider's current terms for the Settings page.
- [ ] Server monitoring and APNs: a small service keyed by anonymous installation id; agree retention (proposed: rules deleted 24 h after their last service date, device tokens on uninstall or 30 days of inactivity).
