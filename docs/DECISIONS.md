# Train Times v1 — build decisions and open questions

The one list every ticket relies on. Decisions are numbered and never renumbered; to change one, strike it through, add the replacement as a new number and say which ticket changed it.

Sources: [`design/handoff/README.md`](../design/handoff/README.md) and [`design/Screen-Requirements.md`](../design/Screen-Requirements.md) (§ Purpose and scope, § Navigation and shared rules, § Settings, § Brand: Train Times, § Visual tokens and accessibility, § Assumptions and open questions). Ticket: TT-100 (#1).

## Decisions

### Platform and project

- **D1. iOS 17 minimum.**
- **D2. SwiftUI** for every screen.
- **D3. Swift 6 language mode**; zero warnings.
- **D4. One app target.** Test targets are separate; the alert server (TT-140) is its own project, not an app target.
- **D5. No login and no account.** Route, recent routes (D48) and settings stay on device; alerts use an anonymous installation id. No payment, subscription, advertising, upgrade or rating prompt anywhere, and no hidden debug menu in release builds.
- **D6. No third-party UI kits.** Every screen is built from `Sources/Design` tokens and components (TT-102). Test-only dependencies such as swift-snapshot-testing are allowed.
- **D7. Display Name is "Train Times".** The App Store name "Train Times: GO train schedule" is set in App Store Connect, not in the project.

### Product

- **D8. The requirements doc wins over the brief.** Where `Screen-Requirements.md` and the Product Design Brief v1 differ, the requirements doc is the source of truth for behaviour; the Schedule screen is a to-scale vertical timeline, not the brief's row list. The design canvas "Train Times (GO train app) — iPhone v1", exported as `design/frames/`, is the source of truth for look; token values come from the handoff (`DesignTokens.swift`, `Colors.xcassets`).
- **D9. Trip identity = service date + feed trip id**, never departure time. A train keeps its name: the 7:32 AM train stays the "7:32 AM train" when its estimate moves to 7:41 AM. Sort by scheduled time, anchor the card at the expected time.
- **D10. No GO Transit or Metrolinx marks** anywhere in the app, its icon, the store screenshots or traintimes.ca, and the Train Times lockup is never shown beside them. The icon never carries letters (a G or GO), GO's olive green, status colours, or an added clock or badge; no lockup on the launch screen, the Schedule navigation title, empty states or notifications; the wordmark is never green text on its own. "GO train" is used only descriptively (store listing, the tagline "Your next GO train, at a glance.", the "Official GO service updates" link), never as the brand. No domain containing go or gotrain.
- **D11. Two tabs only, Schedule and Alerts.** (Superseded by D48: three tabs, Settings is the third.) Settings is pushed from a gear in the Schedule header. Route editing, station search, trip details, alert setup and line notices open as sheets. No splash, tutorial, permission request or carousel before the first timetable.
- **D12. Status is colour + word + icon from a closed set:** On time, Delayed N min, Cancelled, Stop skipped, Scheduled, Last reported HH:MM, Offline. On time needs fresh live evidence or a fresh applicable prediction with a delay under 3 min; Delayed starts at a reported delay of 3 min or more. On time is a neutral badge (green is the brand); only Delayed (amber) and Cancelled / Stop skipped (red) carry colour. (D49 presented a train with no live status as a green On time; D50 reverses that: it reads Scheduled.)
- **D13. Missing or stale data never shows On time.** No live data, or any day other than today, means "Scheduled"; data older than the freshness threshold (Q2) means "Last reported HH:MM". (D49 showed Scheduled as a green On time; D50 restores this rule on the card and Trip details.)
- **D14. Freshness is always visible:** one line stating the data mode (live, scheduled only, stale, offline). A refresh keeps row identity and scroll position. (D49: Schedule shows it as a status row only when loading, offline or stale; Trip details keeps the line.)
- **D15. Every arrival is labelled** "Scheduled arrival" or "Est. arrival"; never a bare arrival time. Destination arrival stays Scheduled until the feed is shown to support an estimate (Q3).
- **D16. Every delay or cancellation shown carries the time it was reported.**
- **D17. One time formatter** for every time in the app, in notifications and in VoiceOver: device 12/24-hour setting by default, overridable in Settings (Device, 12-hour, 24-hour), America/Toronto with DST, tabular digits (TT-106). The alert server needs each device's time format to write notifications, so TT-140 (#22) stores it with the device token.
- **D18. The GO service day:** trips after midnight belong to the previous service day and sort after 11:59 PM.
- **D19. Copy:** sentence case, Canadian spelling, no exclamation marks, no emoji, one fact per sentence. States, never promises: "expected", never "will arrive"; never claim real-time, instant or guaranteed. All strings come from `Localizable.strings`.
- **D20. Fixtures stay fixtures.** Station names, times, platforms and stop lists on the canvas are illustrative; fixtures are isolated from production data and never shown as live service. M0 and M1 are built on fixtures with no network and no API key.
- **D21. Express is a tag, not a status,** and appears only when the feed verifies it (Q4).
- **D22. Accessibility baseline:** status is never colour alone, 44 pt hit targets, Dynamic Type supported through the accessibility sizes (the requirements doc's AX5 wins over the handoff's xxxLarge, because the handoff README names the requirements doc as the source of truth for behaviour), Reduce Motion respected, no countdowns, pulsing dots or flashing.

- **D23. Device and review scope (Grigor, 2026-11-07, TT-101).** Build, test and review on iPhone 17 Pro only. Accessibility (VoiceOver labels, Dynamic Type and large-text layouts) and iPad layout are not reviewed or tested in ticket work, even where an issue's Definition of done lists them; the app stays iPhone only. D22's colour + word + icon status rule and 44 pt targets still apply because they are part of the design.
- **D24. The service day changes at 3:00 AM Toronto time (TT-106).** A moment before 3:00 AM belongs to the previous service day (D18) and sorts by wall-clock minutes plus 24 h, so 00:40 sorts after 23:59. 24-hour times show two-digit hours ("07:32"); 12-hour times show "7:32 AM". Only `Sources/Core/Formatting` may use `DateFormatter` or `Date.formatted`; `scripts/ci-test.sh` fails otherwise.
- **D25. Classic bars, no Liquid Glass (TT-104).** (Reversed by D49: the app now takes Liquid Glass on iOS 26.) `Config/Info.plist` sets `UIDesignRequiresCompatibility` (there is no `INFOPLIST_KEY_` build setting for it) so the tab bar and navigation bars keep the opaque look in the frames: surface tab bar with a hairline, accent selected tab. Apple plans to drop the key in a later SDK; revisit then. Tab icons are drawn in the token colours by `TabBarStyle`, so the Alerts dot is accent and never a number.

The day strip shows 14 days until Q6 sets the horizon. The Now button dims while the timeline already shows the present, as the requirements say, although the frame draws it in accent (D8).

The next usable train is today's only (TT-113).** It is the first train on today's timeline that is not cancelled or skipped and has not reached its departure time; another day has no lead card, no "in N min" and every time at 32 pt. Time pills and gridline labels drop AM and PM ("7:32", or "07:32" in 24-hour). The "Now" label hides within 22 pt of a time pill, as gridline labels do; the line and its dot stay. Every card shows its platform, "Platform pending" included, and an Express tag that does not fit beside it wraps to a second line, as the requirements say, although the frame leaves the 8:21 AM card's platform out (D8).

Station search and the fixture station list (TT-121; the sheet and the anywhere-in-the-name match are replaced by D54).** From and To open the station search sheet (`station-search.png`): a native search field, "Search stations", that matches anywhere in the name ignoring case and accents; then Your stations and All stations, alphabetically. Up to three favourite routes stay on device (`Favourites`, UserDefaults key `route.favourites`), each direction its own; the last route and its direction stay in `route.last` (D28), so a relaunch opens them. - **D31. Delayed and cancelled trains on the timeline (TT-114).** A delayed train's card, marker and pill sit at the expected time and the card reads "Scheduled 7:32 AM" under it; the amber struck pill, hollow amber marker and 4 pt amber bar stay at the scheduled time. It stays the next train until its expected time passes: the scheduled time passing changes nothing (stale data still moves it back to its slot, D13). A cancelled train or skipped stop keeps its scheduled slot; its marker is the badge's icon in red (the frame draws an octagon, `TripStatus` uses `xmark.circle.fill`, so the two match), times struck in ink-tertiary, no platform, no bell, and "Next usable below" only when a usable train follows it. The timeline frames show no reported time, so cards carry none (the ticket's "where the design shows it"); `TrainStatus` keeps `reportedAt` for the notifications that state it (D16). The filled bell in `schedule-delayed.png` is an armed alert (TT-141).
- **D32. Trip details (TT-118).** The sheet loads its own trip, live status and stops (`ScheduleRepository.stops(for:on:)`) and reloads every 30 s while open, so it follows the schedule without holding up the timetable behind it. Estimates follow the timeline's rules (D12, D13): Expected departure and Est. arrival show only while the trip's status rests on fresh live data, and a missing one leaves its cell empty instead of moving another fact into it. The stop list says Expected only when every stop the train calls at has a live time; otherwise every stop shows its scheduled time. "Departed" replaces Expected departure only once the live departure time has passed; the alert button hides once the departure time (live, else scheduled) has passed today. Until alert rules exist (TT-142) the button reads "Alert me". The full stop names in the title come from the stop list; `Fixtures.json` gains the canvas's Lakeshore West stop list, as station ids from its `stations` list with minutes after the origin (D20).
- **D33. Schedule states (TT-115).** "Loading timetable" with three skeleton cards on the rail shows until the first timetable for the route arrives. Stale and offline data show every badge as Scheduled at its scheduled time (D13); offline, every card also reads "Platform pending", because a cached platform may have changed, while a cached cancellation still stands (TT-110). A route with no line (D28) shows the "No direct connection" card, whose "Open GO trip planner" opens https://www.gotransit.com/en/plan-your-trip. A day with no direct trains shows "No direct trains on Sunday"; its "Show Monday" action names the next day in the day strip that has trains and is hidden when none has. Until GTFS lands (TT-130), the fixtures run the canvas timetable, with no live data, on every later day but Sunday, so Sun 8 matches `schedule-no-trains.png`.
- **D34. Dark appearance pass (TT-116).** Under D23 the compact phone layout (6 pt per minute, 32 pt times), the large text layouts and the Increase Contrast and Bold Text adjustments are not built; every size keeps the iPhone 17 Pro layout, and Q9 moves to TT-153 (#30). Dark needed no new tokens: the 21 colours already match the requirements' dark column, and cards drop their shadow in dark. To match the frames, icon buttons (gear, swap, star, bell, close) draw ink-secondary at an 18 pt SF Symbol size as the canvas's `.icon-btn` does, empty-state glyphs are ink-secondary (`.empty`), and a slipped time's amber struck pill is regular weight (`.tl-pill.ghost`). The empty state keeps SF Symbols' `calendar`, the nearest symbol to the canvas's outline calendar, and the stale freshness line may wrap to two lines beside the Now button, because its copy is fixed and 13 pt is the smallest size.
- **D35. The GO GTFS schedule on device (TT-130).** The static feed is public, with no API key: the app downloads `GO-GTFS.zip` from Metrolinx open data (Metrolinx Access and Use Agreement) on first use, imports rail only (route_type 2: stops, routes, trips, stop_times, calendar and calendar_dates) and keeps the last good import on device (`GTFSFeedStore`, Application Support), so the timetable shows offline. It refreshes in the background once the copy is 7 days old; once its last service day has passed, the next use waits for the refresh, since the copy would show no trains. A failed refresh keeps the copy and is tried again after an hour, or after a minute once the copy has run out. With no copy and no network the Schedule keeps "Loading timetable" (pull to refresh retries) rather than saying there are no trains. Times count from noon minus 12 h on the service date, as GTFS defines them, so DST days keep their wall-clock times and 25:10:00 is 1:10 AM the next morning on the same service day (D24). GO publishes each date's trips separately (calendar_dates.txt, one service per date), so a holiday needs no rule of its own. A line is the route's short name ("LW", stable across feeds), long name and colour; a pair two lines serve takes the line with more trains calling at both. Stations are the stops trains call at (71 in the Nov 4 feed), which replaces D29's illustrative list in the app; `Fixtures.json` keeps it for previews and tests (D20). The static feed has no platforms, so every card reads "Platform pending" until live data (TT-131). Debug and Release builds read the feed; Xcode previews, the unit test host and UI tests (`TT_FIXTURES` in the launch environment, `XCUIApplication.onFixtures()`) keep the fixtures, which retires D26's fixture clock in Debug builds.
  - **Express (Q4).** The feed has no express flag (trip_short_name and route_variant are empty). A train is Express between two stations when it skips a station that more than half of its line's trains between those stations call at that day: the Hamilton and Aldershot peak trains that run Clarkson to Union non-stop are Express; off-peak Kitchener trains that skip only Etobicoke North, as about half the trains do, are not.
  - **Horizon (Q6).** The Nov 4 feed covers Nov 4 to 27, about three weeks. The day strip shows today and up to 13 more days, and stops at the feed's last service day; today always shows.
- **D36. Live status from GTFS-realtime, built on sample feeds until the key arrives (TT-131).** GO's TripUpdates endpoint answers 401 without the Metrolinx key, so, at Grigor's request (2026-11-07), live status is built and tested against GTFS-realtime samples for real GO trips, written with the official protobuf bindings (`scripts/make-realtime-samples.py`); Q1 stays open and the mapping is checked against the real feed once the key arrives. The app reads `api/V1/Gtfs/Feed/TripUpdates` as protobuf, decoded in-house (no runtime dependencies, D6), with the key from the `METROLINX_API_KEY` build setting in the git-ignored `Config/Secrets.xcconfig`; a build without it, as every build is today, shows the schedule only. Schedule polls every 30 s while it is on screen in the foreground, at once on coming back to the foreground, and on pull to refresh; Schedule and Trip details share one fetch per 20 s, and no feed is kept across launches.
  - **Mapping.** A train matches on service date + trip id (D9). Cancelled is CANCELED or DELETED; Stop skipped is SKIPPED at the route's origin or destination. The departure estimate is the origin's own estimate, else the delay of the nearest earlier stop carried down the line (GTFS-realtime's rule), else the trip-wide delay; NO_DATA, or no applicable estimate, leaves the train Scheduled. Est. arrival needs the destination's own arrival estimate (Q3). Trip details' stops show only each stop's own estimate or skip.
  - **Freshness.** Data age counts from the feed's own timestamp, so a feed that stops updating turns stale after 3 min (Q2) even while fetches succeed; data on screen is resolved again before each fetch, so coming back to the app never shows old data as live while it reloads; a failed fetch (10 s timeout) keeps the last feed as offline. TripUpdates carry no platforms, so cards keep "Platform pending".
  - **To check with the key (Q1):** the `key` parameter name, that trip ids and start dates match the static feed, that stop updates carry stop ids, that a cancelled trip stays in the feed for the rest of its day, the update interval (Q2), destination estimates (Q3), how estimates move trains on the timeline (Q10), the key's terms (Q7), and whether a key built into the app is allowed or the feed should come through the alert server (TT-140).
- **D37. Line notices from GTFS-realtime service alerts, built on sample feeds until the key arrives (TT-132).** As with live status (D36), GO's Alerts endpoint (`api/V1/Gtfs/Feed/Alerts`) needs the Metrolinx key, so line notices are built and tested against GTFS-realtime Alerts samples for real GO routes, written with the official protobuf bindings (`scripts/make-realtime-samples.py`); Q5 stays open until the scoping is checked against the real feed. The app reads the Alerts feed only when built with the key, so a build without it shows no notice, as before.
  - **Scope.** An alert is a notice for a line when one of its informed entities names one of the line's routes and no trip (a stop may narrow it); an alert about a trip or a station alone is not a line notice. Routes match on the code after GO's schedule-period prefix (`09261126-LW` is `LW`; the Kitchener line's route is `GT`), because route ids change from one feed to the next and the schedule on device can be a week old (D35); the line's own id matches too. A notice shows while one of its active periods holds the current time (always, with none), on today only: another day shows none, as it shows no live status. Its text is the English header (else the untranslated or first one), and the sheet adds the description as the full notice; its reported time is the start of its active period, else the feed's time.
  - **Never a status.** Notices are read apart from the trips and never change a status, the next train or a card; only TripUpdates do (D36). The samples include a NO_SERVICE alert for one trip, and that trip stays Scheduled.
  - **Link and sheet.** "· Line notice" in delay-ink with the notice icon follows the freshness text, as in `schedule-delayed.png`, and opens the line notice sheet: "Line notice" with Close, then each notice in the canvas's disruption banner (`components.png`: "Lakeshore West notice", the notice, "Reported at 7:12 AM") with its dismiss button. Dismissing hides that notice id on device (`DismissedNotices`, UserDefaults key `notices.dismissed`, the newest 50 ids), after a relaunch too, until a notice with a new id arrives; the sheet closes once no notice is left. With stale or offline text the freshness line wraps to two lines beside the link, as D34 allows. Notices load alongside live status, follow Schedule's 30 s poll and the 20 s fetch reuse; a failed fetch keeps the last good feed's notices.
  - **To check with the key (Q5):** that GO publishes line-wide notices with route ids and in which form, whether agency-wide alerts (no route) should count as notices for every line, that a notice keeps its id while it is unchanged, and that active periods carry a start worth showing as "Reported at".
- **D38. The alert server (TT-140).** A Cloudflare Worker with a D1 database in `server/` (its own project, D4), on the Cloudflare account that already serves traintimes.ca, at alerts.traintimes.ca: the smallest of the three options, with a cron and storage built in. It runs on the Workers Free plan (D43). It has no runtime dependencies: the GTFS-realtime decoder and live mapping are ports of the app's (D36), and APNs uses a .p8 key with an ES256 provider token signed by WebCrypto. Setup: `server/README.md`.
  - **API.** Keyed by an anonymous installation id from the app (D5). `PUT /v1/installations/:id` registers the device token, the app's Time format resolved to 12h or 24h (D17) and the APNs environment. Rules are saved and deleted with `PUT` and `DELETE /v1/installations/:id/rules/:rule`; saving the same rule id again updates it. `GET …/rules` lists them with state On or Review needed and the last day with no matching train. A rule carries the picked train (trip id, service date, line, scheduled departure) and the origin and destination ids and names, so notifications name the stations as the app does. A bad registration gets 400 with the field at fault; a rule for an unknown installation gets 404.
  - **Monitoring.** Every minute, a trip is watched from 60 min before its scheduled departure to 2 h after, against one TripUpdates fetch. The alert rules follow § Notification rules and copy, with these details. Nothing is sent within 60 s of a trip's last alert; the next cycle sends the latest status, which coalesces rapid changes. After a recovery, a new delay alert also needs the delay to move 5 min from the one the recovery reported, so an estimate wobbling around 3 min sends one alert and one recovery. Each send is a compare-and-set on the trip's sent state, so overlapping cycles, or a This-trip and a Repeat rule for the same train, never send twice. A feed older than 3 min (Q2) starts nothing. A reliable departure is the feed reporting the origin departure at or before its own timestamp; time passing alone is not one, and watching ends at the 2 h expiry. The APNs expiry is the scheduled departure plus 2 h. The payload adds `tripId` and `serviceDate`, or `ruleId` for Review needed. The requirements table's "now delayed" title for a meaningful change is added to the strings as `notif.delay.update.title`. Line notice notifications (`notif.line.*`) wait for TT-132's feed.
  - **Repeating rules.** The server has the rail timetable for today and the next 7 days from the public GTFS zip, imported once a day (D35) by GitHub Actions (D43). The usual train on a service date is the trip on the same line that leaves the origin at the same scheduled time and then calls at the destination. If it does not run today but runs on another selected weekday in the next 7 days, today is an exception: "No matching train today", and nothing is sent. If it runs on none of them, the rule is set to Review needed and one "Your usual train has changed" notification is sent. The rule is never attached to another train.
  - **Failures.** APNs 410 means the app was deleted, so its installation and rules are removed (rule 9). BadDeviceToken clears the token until the app registers again, and the alert is decided again then. 429, 5xx, network errors and an expired provider token are retried on the next cycle.
  - **Retention (closes Q8; Grigor, 2026-11-07).** A This-trip rule and its sent state are deleted 24 h after its service day ends. A repeat rule stays until it is deleted. An installation is removed when APNs says the app was deleted, or after 30 days without the app calling the server, unless it has an active repeat alert (On, not paused), so a commuter who relies on a weekday alert keeps it.
  - **Without the Metrolinx key** there is no live feed, so nothing is sent. The TT-131 sample feeds replay the server end to end in its tests (`cd server && npm test`). Real delivery on a device waits for the APNs key, the deployment and the app's registration (TT-142).
- **D39. Notifications keep the default sound and interruption level, and the app adds no foreground presentation.
  - **Watched-trip strip.** It shows for today's watched train (a trip with an alert, read through `WatchedTripsProviding`, which the alert rules of TT-141 and TT-142 provide) from the moment its live departure time has passed, the rule Trip details uses for "Departed" (D32), until 10 min after its arrival, estimated or else scheduled and labelled as such (D15), or until it is swiped sideways, which hides it while the app runs. With several, the latest departure shows. With no live data, or once the feed no longer carries the trip, there is no strip: check with the key whether a trip stays in the feed until it arrives (Q1).

A tap opens the train's Trip details (`traintimes://departure`). **Who starts it.** The alert server, with a push-to-start (iOS 17.2+) at T−30 for every alerted train whose installation sent a push-to-start token, with the alert "Your 5:13 PM train is live" (`depart.live.title`) and the route, sent once; on iOS 17.0 and 17.1 only the app starts it. The app in front starts it itself when none came (it waits 90 s after T−30 when it holds a push-to-start token, so the two do not cross; if both start, the app ends the second one and reports the token of the one it keeps), with an update token, once per train while it runs (one the rider swiped away stays away; one swiped away while the app was closed can come back when the app is opened in the window, since the app cannot tell it from one that never started). Either way it ends 2 min after the expected departure: its stale date, which the app enforces, and the server's `end`. **Tokens.** The push-to-start token goes with the installation's registration (`startToken`, only once the rider has an alert); each departure activity reports itself to `PUT /v1/installations/:id/activities` (trip, service date, stations, its update token when iOS issues it, `handedOff` after the hand-off, sent again on each pass until the server has it, or its `end` would close the ride's activity), through `AlertRules` or, in a background launch, straight from `RideLiveActivity`. **The server.** `server/src/activity.ts` writes every word the app writes for the same train (GO's feeds post no platforms, so it always says "Check the board"); without live status it starts on the timetable ("Scheduled"), so this is the one push the server sends before the Metrolinx key arrives. `update` (priority 10) only when what a rider sees changes (phase, title, detail, ends, the countdown to the half minute; not the update time), `end` once the train is 2 min gone or cancelled; nothing after the app reports the hand-off. All within the minute cycle's 45 sends (D43); a send APNs asks to retry is tried again by the next cycle, a token APNs rejects is dropped. Tables `start_tokens` and `activities` (rerun `schema.sql`); kept as long as the rule's service date.
